# Applying BABOK to Real Businesses

A second track alongside technical writing: applying BABOK v3 to real, early-stage companies rather than case-study exercises.

This section covers Business Analysis work I've taken on: stakeholder analysis, process modeling, requirements definition, and risk analysis, applied to actual companies rather than training scenarios. The two disciplines share a common thread. Both are about translating complexity into something clear enough for the right audience to act on, whether that audience is a developer reading documentation or a founder trying to see their own process for the first time.

It's also a natural extension of my interest in AI-driven systems. The case study below is for an AI-powered platform, and the same questions I ask when documenting how an AI system works, who relies on it, what breaks if a step is skipped, where the human checkpoints actually need to sit, turned out to be exactly the questions a business analyst asks when defining how that system should be built and governed in the first place.

This list will grow as more engagements are completed.

## Case Studies

**Lirafin: Applying BABOK to an Early-Stage Startup**, stakeholder analysis, process modeling, requirements classification, and root-cause risk analysis for an early-stage AI-powered fintech platform, broken into four phases:

1. [Stakeholder Analysis](stakeholder-analysis.md), identifying who has a stake in the business and designing engagement around influence and interest
2. [Process Modeling](process-modeling.md), a swimlane diagram, full process specification, and a RACI matrix for the content pipeline
3. [Risk & Requirements](risk-and-requirements.md), a Five Whys root-cause analysis, a consolidated risk register, and a formal requirements classification
4. [User Stories & Use Cases](user-stories-and-use-cases.md), a SIPOC summary, a MoSCoW-prioritised backlog, an INVEST quality check, and a UML use case diagram

Each page includes the actual artifacts produced, diagrams, registers, and matrices, not just a summary of the approach.
